## Abstract
### Abstract
Trajectory planning and trajectory tracking control are the key problems of intelligent vehicle autonomous driving system. The traditional control method cannot simultaneously achieve precision,robustness, and constraint satisfaction in the presence of a complex environment and model uncertainties. Although Model Predictive Control (MPC) is able to deal with multiple constraints explicitly,its performance is very much dependent on the model's accuracy. The Learning-based MPC (LB-MPC), which combines the advantages of machine learning and MPC by using a data-driven manner to build or improve the prediction model,thus, it can greatly improve the robustness and accuracy of the control with security. Therefore, this paper provides an overview of methodology development and improvement theory on LB-MPC in intelligent driving path tracking. The main contribution is the following: (i) A hierarchical categorization into three levels for improved structure of LB-MPC with learning, controller paramter optimization, and safety integration mechanism;(2) formal mathematical analysis on the trade-offs between model accuracy and computation complexity in real-time control scenario;(3) rigorous analysis of the performance and robustness to unseen scenarios. Comparative literature review reveals that the LB-MPC methods achieve a performance gain of about 15%-40% over the standard MPC with respect to the tracking accuracy when model uncertainties are present:while keeping the rate of constraint violations above 95% for challenging problems. Yet it comes with an increase of 20-300% computation time according to the learning model's complexity,and their generalization performance degrades by 25-60% out of the training distribution. This systematic review points to the main open problems for safety verification, online adaptation, and theoretical stability guaranteeswhich can be used as the starting point to develop an autonomous driving controller.

## 1 Introduction
Trajectory generation and tracking are key problems for guaranteeing the safety and ride comfort of autonomous vehicles \ [1\], where trajectory generation creates feasible trajectories which satisfies vehicle dynamics constraint and road constraint based on environment information and drive objective,while trajectory tracking means that the vehicle should be controlled along with a given trajectory, and keep the deviation error in an appropriate range. Traditional approaches including the use of feedback linearisation, sliding mode control, and even classical optimal control have had some measure of success under particular circumstances,but they still fall short of performing well on complicated and ambiguous scenarios \ [2\].
The idea of Model Predictive Control (MPC) is to solve an optimisation over a finite time horizon in real-time, i.e., at every sampling period; it computes the best possible control action at the present time-step with a receding horizon approach and recomputes this optimal control from updated state information at next sampling period. In comparison with conventional control method, MPC could explicitly deal with state and control input constraint in the course of optimization and realize the coordinated control on multi-input multioutput coupling system. With the introduction of reference trajectory, MPC could also incorporate preview path information in the control objective for better curved path tracking performances. Variants, like robust MPC or stochastic MPC add more strength in handling model uncertainty and environmental stochasticity.
But the control performance of MPC relies heavily on the accuracy of the controlled object model. If there are some uncertainties in the vehicle dynamic model, simplification errors, or if it suffers from any form of exogenous disturbance then the prediction accuracy and optimization performance of MPC suffer significantly \ [3\]. Under the condition of severe operation such as high speed running and turning movement, the motion of vehicles has a strong nonlinearity characteristic, and the road environment is complicated and dynamic, so that system dynamics cannot be fully characterized by pre-modeling \ [4\]. Conventional MPC usually employs a reducedorder linearized model or a low-order non-linear model, while it is convenient to solve in real time, have intrinsic shortcomings to describe complex factors like tire cornering characteristic, suspension dynamics, and the aerodynamic loads. Moreover, the values of model parameters change according to different values of road surface adhesion coefficient, distribution of loads acting on a vehicle and other environmental factors, leading to large discrepancies between the nominal model and the true plant.
Modeling errors have different influence in the control performances under different operation points. In particular, they have a more relevant effect when the vehicle is driven at low velocities (less than 60 km/h, lateral acceleration < 0.3g) the error from linearised bicycle models are usually less than 10 - 15cm laterally, or 2 - 3 degrees in heading \ [4\]. However, in more dynamic situations – such as emergency lane changes on highways at high speed or cornering at the limit of tire adhesion – model-plant mismatch causes tracking error to increase by a factor of up to fourfold, with the linearization errors themselves accounting for up to half of the error \ [5\]\ [6\].
To overcome these issues, recently there has been research to integrate Machine Learning (ML) in the control loop, combining it with MPC for a Learning-Based Model Predictive Control (LB-MPC) \ [7\]. ML techniques could be used to automatically identify the unknown parts of vehicle model or time varying features by using history data as well as online measurements, continuously refining the predictive model of MPC \ [8\]. As another example, researchers have been able to achieve a stable trajectory tracking of quadrotor unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) flights even when faced with uncertain disturbance using an approach that learns the uncertainty from models on-line via Extended Kalman Filtering and uses it in conjunction with MPC to find optimal control inputs at each time-step. LB-MPC organically combines statistical learning and control theory, improving the control performance on complex environment with guarantee of safety and robustness theoretically. Existing work has shown that the performance of LB-MPC approaches is on-par with, and in some cases better than existing optimal control, while dramatically reducing the engineering effort for model tuning and controller design.
This article has reviewed the methodology as well as recent progress on learning based MPC for intelligent vehicles’ trajectory generation and tracking from a systematic perspective. In this article, we firstly introduce the formal problem description and vehicle dynamic model, explain the mathematical optimisation formulation of MPC, where we next give an in-depth categorization on learning-based MPC augmentation frameworks including model learning and controller parameters tuning, and safety verification techniques. Then compares the strengths and weaknesses of learning based MPC in terms of generalization and performance properties, and finally addresses some major issues and open problems of the existing literature.

## 3 MPC Control Principle and Optimization Framework
MPC implements predictive control through receding horizon optimization. At discrete time instant k, given the current state \\({x}\_k\\), MPC solves an optimization problem with prediction horizon length N:

(4)
\\begin{equation} \\begin{array}{@{}l@{}} {\\rm{s}}{\\rm{.t}}{\\rm{. }}{{\\rm{x}}}\_{{\\rm{i + 1}}}{\\rm{ = f(}}{{\\rm{x}}}\_{\\rm{i}}{\\rm{,}}{{\\rm{u}}}\_{\\rm{i}}{\\rm{), }}{{\\rm{x}}}\_{\\rm{0}}{\\rm{ = }}{{\\rm{x}}}\_{\\rm{k}}{\\rm{,}}\\\ {\\rm{ }}{{\\rm{x}}}\_{\\rm{i}} \\in {\\rm{X,}}{{\\rm{u}}}\_{\\rm{i}} \\in {\\rm{U, }}{{\\rm{x}}}\_{\\rm{N}} \\in {{\\rm{X}}}\_{\\rm{f}}{\\rm{\ \ }} \\end{array} \\end{equation}
(5)
Where Q and R are stage cost weights, and P is the terminal cost weight. The objective function penalizes state tracking errors and control effort, with the terminal term ensuring long-term performance. After solving the optimization problem to obtain the optimal control sequence, MPC adopts a receding horizon strategy: only the first control \\(u\_0^\*\\), is executed, and the problem is re-solved at the next time instant based on new state feedback \ [12\].
To ensure closed-loop stability, appropriate design of the terminal constraint set \\({{\\rm{X}}}\_{\\rm{f}}\\) and terminal cost \\({\\rm{P}}\\) is required. For linearized models, the optimization problem is transformed into Quadratic Programming (QP); for nonlinear models, Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) is employed. In practice, techniques such as model order reduction, adaptive adjustment of prediction horizon, and specialized solvers are commonly adopted to enhance computational efficiency \ [11\].
## 4 Learning-BasedMPC Method Classification and Enhancement Structures
Learning-based MPC introduces machine learning techniques to continuously improve model prediction and control decisions within the control loop, thereby enhancing system performance and intelligence \ [3\]. ### 4.1 Learning-Driven Predictive Model Optimization
The accuracy of the predictive model is crucial to the performance of MPC. Traditional vehicle models based on first principles are often simplified and linearized, making it difficult to fully describe complex vehicle dynamics and tire nonlinear characteristics, and model parameters vary under different road surfaces and load conditions. This has prompted researchers to adopt machine learning methods to construct data-driven predictive models. A typical approach is to decompose the vehicle model into a nominal part and an uncertain part: \\({{\\rm{x}}}\_{{\\rm{k + 1}}}{\\rm{ = }}{{\\rm{f}}}\_{{\\rm{nom}}}{\\rm{(}}{{\\rm{x}}}\_{\\rm{k}}{\\rm{,}}{{\\rm{u}}}\_{\\rm{k}}{\\rm{) + \\Delta f(}}{{\\rm{x}}}\_{\\rm{k}}{\\rm{,}}{{\\rm{u}}}\_{\\rm{k}}{\\rm{)}}\\). By collecting vehicle operational data, learning models (such as Gaussian Process GP, Neural Network NN) are trained to approximate \\({\\rm{\\Delta f}}\\), achieving correction of the true dynamics \ [10\]. In the "cautious MPC" method proposed by Camps et al., GP is used to learn model residuals online and provide uncertainty estimates, which are incorporated into MPC optimization to improve robustness \ [5\]. The advantage of the GP method lies in its ability to simultaneously provide prediction mean and variance, enabling MPC to consider confidence intervals of model uncertainty during the optimization process, thereby generating more conservative but safe control strategies. Other studies have adopted Bayesian linear regression to replace GP for modeling unmodeled parts, demonstrating better accuracy and generalization capability in mean estimation and uncertainty quantification. To address the lack of physical constraints in purely data-driven models, researchers commonly employ gray-box modeling approaches. These methods retain the fundamental structure of vehicle dynamics while using learning models to compensate for unmodeled effects. This hybrid structure provides both physical interpretability and data-driven flexibility. Furthermore, transfer learning and incremental learning mechanisms can shorten model training time and improve data efficiency: pre-training models using simulation or historical data, then fine-tuning on new vehicles to adapt to new dynamics characteristics, or continuously updating model parameters online to track system changes \ [13\]\ [14\]. Learning-based model optimization endows MPC with adaptive capability, enabling the controller to maintain good performance by updating the predictive model even when environmental or vehicle operating conditions change \ [15\].
### 4.2 Learning Optimization of Controller Design and Parameters
This category of methods focuses on using learning algorithms to automatically adjust MPC controller design elements. A typical direction is cost function learning, which can employ inverse reinforcement learning or imitation learning to learn implicit driving preferences from expert driving data and integrate them into MPC cost function weight configurations \ [16\]. MPC terminal constraint sets and terminal costs significantly affect closed-loop stability. In recent years, research has proposed terminal component learning frameworks: learning terminal stability constraint sets \\({{\\rm{X}}}\_{\\rm{f}}\\) and corresponding terminal value function approximations \\({{\\rm{V}}}\_{\\rm{f}}{\\rm{(x)}}\\) through iterative trial run data \ [12\]. Additionally, by leveraging deep neural networks to learn the state-control mapping relationship of MPC offline, the online computational burden can be significantly reduced with minimal performance loss \ [17\].
### 4.3 Safety-Integrated Learning Control Architecture
Combining learning-based control strategies with the MPC framework represents another important development direction of LB-MPC \ [10\]. A common form is the safety filter: candidate control actions are provided by learning control strategies (such as deep reinforcement learning policies), and then MPC performs online correction or verifies their safety \ [18\]. MPC acts as a safety net, promptly adjusting control inputs when candidate actions may violate constraints, ensuring that the vehicle always remains within the safe operating domain. Moreover, LB-MPC can also be manifested in multi-module hierarchical control architectures: the upper layer utilizes learning algorithms for high-level path planning, while the lower layer executes specific trajectory tracking through MPC. This architecture combines the global decision-making capability of artificial intelligence with the stable execution capability of MPC \ [19\].

Some remarks can be made from this comparison: (i) all the proposed LB-MPC controllers have better performance than the standard MPC controller when there is uncertainty on models, reducing root-mean-square tracking error (RMSE) by 15-40% \ [4\]\ [5\]. The improvement was greatest for aggressive maneuvers: GP-augmented MPC achieved 38% lower lateral deviation than nominal MPC while attempting to make a lane change quickly (> 80 km/h), without constraint violation \ [4\].
Implementation methods have different compute implications, with neural networks and other predictive models requiring up to three times as much computation than an analytical model, with the forward propagation time scaling almost linearly in terms of network depth \ [17\]. For comparison, the overhead for the GPbased approaches increases by 20-80%, although the cost of kernel matrix operations may be prohibitive past 500 - 1000 training points \ [7\]\ [10\]. However, it is important to keep in mind that the real-time capability also has to be taken into account: although the learned approach can be deployed on conventional automotive computer hardware (computation time 15 - 45 ms)online adaptive approaches typically rely on special-purpose hardware or model order reduction for less than a 50 ms control cycles \ [11\]\ [14\].
Another important trade-offis generalization. Performance degradation was reported to be 25-60% for LB-MPC when running outof-distribution from its training data \ [5\]\ [10\]. As an example, a GP-MPC controller trained on dry asphalt (μ ≈ 0.9) showed an increase of 52% in the tracking error if used directly for wet asphalt (μ ≈ 0.5), i.e., with no re-training, compared to only 31% degradation in the case of robust MPC with conservatively-designed uncertainty bounds \ [3\]. This calls for a large coverage of the training data, or an online adaptation procedure.

### 5.2 Generalization Capability and Performance Characteristics
LB-MPC can achieve better trajectory tracking accuracy for cases that are highly nonlinear and have more complicated operation regimes via the data driven optimization of models and controllers \ [4\]. LB-MPC is able to realize control closer to performance limits, yet keeping safe constraints, balancing between the rigour of an optimal control and the flexibility of ML. The lack of generalisation ability in LB-MPC is still a dominant concern \ [1\]. The shortcomings are mainly reflected as: (1) poor generalizability for data-driven models—the learned model usually performs well just on the training data distribution, and its model accuracy as well as control performance will degrade greatly once it comes across with some new road curves that have not been experienced before, or under extremely bad environment \ [10\].(2) heavy reliance on the amount of data—the construction of accurate learning models usually demands a considerable number of labelled data \ [5\];(3) online problems —the introduction of complicated learning models increases the computational burden of every optimization;(4) lack of complete theory on learning-in-the-loopclosed loop system—theory for stability, convergence is mature in conventional MPC;the resulting system is timevarying or adaptive due the inclusion of learning and thus classical control theory cannot be applied straightforwardly \ [13\].
To improve the generalization capability of LB-MPC, possible improvements are: hybrid modelling which combines physical priors and data driven techniques in order to increase the range where models can be applied, developing effective transfer learning and online learning mechanism for quickly adapting to a new situation, developing uncertainty quantification mechanisms that improve the robustness towards unknown states, and using simulators as a way of doing massive scale training, covering more long-tail scenarios \ [19\].
## 6 Research Trends
Learning-based MPC as a new trend for trajectory tracking in the domain of intelligent driving systems remains many problems to be studied further and deeply. First, predictive models which, once machine learning is introduced, tend to be high-dimensional black boxes that are not physically interpretable, leading to a problem of verifying the safety \ [10\]. Constructing provable learning control systems e.g., by switching to grey-box model and enforcing the physics constraint or utilizing MPC safe filter which guarantees no violation of the safety constraint along the trajectory;is one of the directions to solve that problem. Secondly, due to present-day issue of big-data requirement for LB-MPC, we need to consider samplesaving learning approaches, including transfer learning, domain adaptation, and safe and effi- cient exploration for RL \ [16\]. Simulators could be used to generate a lot of training data, and simulation to reality transfer methods can be used for closing the simulation to reality gap.
In addition, the vehicle will have some phenomenon like components aging and load changing with time pass by, so that LB-MPC should be equipped with the ability of continuous online learning and model updating \ [20\]. Computational burden of LB-MPC under complicated condition is another vital limitation. Future directions include decreasing the online optimisation complexity of MPC on an algorithmic level e.g., via decomposition of largescale optimisation problems using distributed computation, or through deep neural networks approximating MPC optimization policies off-line \ [21\]. In parallel, a complete theory of learningbased MPC is still lacking.develop control system analysis tools for systems with learning model based upon robust control and probabilistic control theory, such as combining Lyapunov methods and statistical confidence bounds for evaluating closedloop stability \ [9\]. Finally, autonomous driving trajectory control often involves trade-offs between many objectives including path tracking accuracy, comfortability of ride, and fuel efficiency. The automatic adjustment of the multi-objective weights via learning approaches, so as to reach a dynamical balance between various control objectives, is another urgent open issue that needs to be addressed.
## 7 Conclusion
This paper has reviewed the methodological structure and theoretical improvement of learning based MPC for intelligent vehicles’ trajectory planning and tracking task, by introducing mathematical modelling and optimal control formulation, the paper explained the basic concepts of MPC, as well as different methods to integrate ML in it, such as data-based model correction, controller parameter learning, or safety-integrated control. Learning based MPC couples the ability for ML to characterize complex systems and the optimal decision making capability of model predictive control, which demonstrates good trajectory tracking performance with adaptability to the uncertainty environment, butlacks in the aspect of generality and security guarantee. With further integration of machine learning and control theory, learning-based model predictive control is expected to become a cornerstone technology for intelligent vehicle control systems.
